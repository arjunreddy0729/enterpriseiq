NORTHWIND SYSTEMS - ON-CALL ROTATION HANDBOOK
Maintained by: Sofia Reyes, Engineering Manager, Infrastructure
Contact: sofia.reyes@northwind.example

This handbook covers the mechanics of being on call: who is on, when they hand
off, what wakes them up, and how to swap out. What happens once something is
broken is a different document - see incident-response.md for severities, roles,
and the postmortem process. This file assumes you already know that a SEV1 has a
15-minute ack target and a SEV2 has 30 minutes.

ROTATION SHAPE

    Rotations are WEEKLY. Each participating team runs its own, with exactly
    two people on at a time:

        PRIMARY     - receives all pages first. Owns the response.
        SECONDARY   - backstop. Paged if the primary has not acknowledged
                      within the ack target for that severity. Also the first
                      person the primary pulls in for a second pair of hands.

    The secondary is not decoration. On a SEV1 the primary will be busy being
    Incident Commander or debugging, and the secondary is who actually gets
    things done. Treat a week as secondary as a real on-call week. A person is
    never primary and secondary in the same week, and never primary on two
    teams in the same week.

    Teams currently running a rotation:

        Platform            (api-gateway, identity-service)
        Payments            (payments-service, ledger-service)
        Shipments           (shipment-service)
        Infrastructure      (cluster, databases, CI/CD, and SEV1 backstop)

    Infrastructure is paged in parallel on any SEV1, not sequentially.

HANDOFF

    Handoff is MONDAY AT 10:00 AMERICA/CHICAGO. That time is deliberate:
    inside business hours for Austin and Denver, late afternoon in Dublin, so
    both engineers overlap live regardless of office. Do not move it to Friday
    afternoon - handing an unstable system to someone at the start of a weekend
    is how a SEV3 becomes a SEV1.

    Handoff is a real conversation, not a calendar event that passes silently.
    Fifteen minutes, both people present, outgoing primary drives.

    HANDOFF CHECKLIST - outgoing primary walks through each item:

        [ ] Open incidents. Anything still running, and who is IC.
        [ ] Incidents closed this week that are not fully resolved underneath.
            "It stopped happening" is not "we fixed it."
        [ ] Temporary mitigations still in place - a lowered rate limit, a
            disabled job, a feature turned off, a pinned image. Each gets an
            owner and a removal date. This is the item people forget, and the
            item that causes the most trouble.
        [ ] Alerts that fired: which were real, which were noise, which are
            still firing. Noisy alerts go to the weekly operations meeting;
            see observability-standards.md.
        [ ] Deploys that landed, and anything scheduled for the coming week.
            Check the incoming week for a deploy freeze: nothing goes to
            production after 16:00 Friday, and nothing December 20 through 31.
        [ ] Anything running long - a backfill, a migration soak, a
            reconciliation catching up. Plus known-flaky things and open
            postmortem action items touching this service.
        [ ] Confirm the incoming primary and secondary are reachable. Send a
            real test page, every rotation. A paging setup that silently broke
            three weeks ago is only ever discovered at 02:00.

PAGE OR TICKET

    Getting this line right is the single biggest factor in whether on-call at
    Northwind is sustainable. The rule:

        A PAGE means a human must act NOW, at any hour.
        A TICKET means a human must act during business hours.

    PAGE if:
        - Customers cannot complete a core flow: authenticating, creating a
          payment, creating a shipment.
        - Money is at risk. Payment loss is a SEV1 by definition.
        - A service is down or failing health checks in production.
        - Error rate or latency has breached the service objective, sustained.
        - Webhook signature verification is failing. A nonzero rate means a
          wrong secret or a forgery attempt, and both are urgent.
        - Anything that would be a SEV1 or SEV2.

    TICKET if:
        - A single tenant is affected by a bounded, non-money bug.
        - A background job is behind but catching up.
        - Saturation is elevated but not yet a problem.
        - A certificate expires in three weeks, or a dashboard is wrong.
        - Anything that would be a SEV3 or SEV4.

    NEITHER - fix the alert - if there was never going to be anything to do, or
    if it fires on a cause rather than a symptom. Nobody experiences a pod
    restart; users experience failed checkouts. If you are paged for something
    that should have been a ticket, reclassify it that week. See
    observability-standards.md.

ESCALATION LADDER

    Rung 1   PRIMARY on-call for the owning team. Paged immediately.
    Rung 2   SECONDARY on-call, same team. Paged automatically if the primary
             has not acknowledged within the ack target for that severity:
             15 minutes for a SEV1, 30 minutes for a SEV2.
    Rung 3   TEAM MANAGER. Paged if the secondary has also not acknowledged.
    Rung 4   ENGINEERING LEADERSHIP. Notified by the Incident Commander on any
             SEV1 at declaration, and on any SEV2 running past 60 minutes
             without a clear mitigation path.

    Sideways at any point:
        - Infrastructure on-call, for anything cluster, database, or deploy
          related. Paged in parallel on every SEV1 regardless.
        - Legal, if there is any possibility personal data is involved. Tom
          Lindqvist, General Counsel, and Rina Malhotra, Privacy Counsel. A
          72-hour customer notification obligation attaches to confirmed
          security incidents involving personal data, and that clock is not
          something to reason about alone at 02:00.

    Escalating early is correct behavior. Stuck for fifteen minutes on a SEV1
    is a reason to add people, not to try harder alone.

COMPENSATION AND TIME BACK

    On-call is compensated. The specifics - eligibility, the rate, and how it
    is paid out - are set by People Operations and documented in the HR
    compensation policy, owned by Marcus Webb, Director of People Operations.
    Deliberately no figures here: this file would go stale, and the HR policy
    is the source of truth.

    What this handbook does commit to:
        - If you were paged outside working hours and it materially cost you
          sleep, take the time back. Tell your manager, do not ask permission.
        - An engineer running on four hours of sleep making changes to the
          money path is a risk to the platform, not a hero.
        - Repeated bad weeks on one rotation are a systems problem and get
          fixed as one, with engineering time allocated at the weekly
          operations meeting. Not by rotating the suffering.

SWAPPING SHIFTS

    Swapping is normal and easy. The rules exist only to keep the schedule
    matching reality.

        1. Find your own cover. Ask the team channel; do not ask your manager
           to find someone for you.
        2. Both people update the on-call schedule directly. The schedule is
           the source of truth for who gets paged.
        3. Post the swap in the team channel, and notify your manager if it
           crosses a handoff boundary or covers a weekend.
        4. Record swaps before they take effect. One arranged verbally on
           Friday and never entered will page the wrong person on Saturday.

    Partial swaps - a single night, a single day - are fine on the same rules.

    PLANNED TIME OFF goes in as far ahead as you can so the rotation is built
    around it. You should not be on call while on PTO. If you cannot find
    cover, escalate to your manager; being nominally on call while unreachable
    is much worse than saying so in advance.

    NEW JOINERS shadow one full rotation as a third, non-paged participant
    before taking a primary week.
