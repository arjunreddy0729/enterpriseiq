> or summarise this document to non-managers. If you are unsure whether someone
> should have it, they should not.

## Why This Process Exists

Reviews serve three purposes: **feedback**, telling people accurately where they
stand; **calibration**, making "meets expectations" mean the same thing on the
Payments team as it does on Infrastructure; and **input to pay and promotion**,
since ratings feed the merit cycle and the promotion committee described in
compensation-policy.md. A review that surprises someone has already failed —
nothing written in a review should be the first time an employee hears it.

## 1. Cadence

Reviews run **twice yearly, in April and October**. The two cycles differ.

| | Spring cycle | Autumn cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Review month | April | October |
| Ratings issued | Yes | Yes |
| Feeds merit increases | **Yes** — effective 1 April | No |
| Feeds promotion committee | Yes — March committee | Yes — October committee |
| Calibration | March | September |
| Typical emphasis | Outcomes and compensation | Development and trajectory |

Merit increases happen **once a year, effective 1 April**, with calibration in
March. The autumn cycle carries no automatic pay change; its only compensation
consequence is a promotion decided by the October committee, effective 1 November.

### 1.1 Spring cycle timeline

| Week | Activity |
|------|----------|
| Late January | Cycle opens; managers confirm reviewer rosters |
| Early February | Self-reviews due |
| Mid February | Peer feedback due |
| Late February | Draft ratings and merit recommendations entered |
| March, weeks 1–3 | Calibration sessions |
| Late March | Approvals routed; final ratings locked |
| Final week of March | Delivery conversations, live, 1:1 |
| 1 April | Merit increases effective |

The autumn cycle mirrors this two months earlier, minus the merit steps.

### 1.2 Eligibility

Employees who started **fewer than 90 days** before the review month are not
rated; they get an unscored written check-in that does not enter calibration.
Employees on leave during the cycle are rated on the period they worked, with no
proration penalty — see leave-policy.md.

## 2. The Rating Scale

A **5-point scale**. Use the whole of it. A manager whose entire team lands on 3
has not calibrated; they have avoided a conversation.

| Rating | Label | Definition |
|--------|-------|------------|
| **5** | Exceptional | Delivered outcomes materially beyond the level's scope, repeatedly, across the whole period. The work changed what the team or company could do. Others cite it as the standard. Rare — one or two on a large team, often zero. |
| **4** | Exceeds expectations | Consistently delivered above what the level requires. Took on ambiguity without being asked. A credible promotion case within a cycle or two. |
| **3** | Meets expectations | Delivered what the level requires, reliably. **This is a good rating.** It describes someone doing the job they are paid to do, well. The majority of the company sits here and should. |
| **2** | Partially meets expectations | Delivered some of what the level requires, with meaningful and specific gaps. Needs a documented improvement plan next period, though not necessarily a formal PIP. |
| **1** | Does not meet expectations | Sustained failure to deliver at level despite clear, documented feedback. A formal performance improvement plan is required. |

Two standing rules. **3 is not a failure** — managers who apologise for a 3 are
the reason employees read it as one. And **a 1 or a 2 must never be new
information**: if the concern was not raised in writing during the period, the
rating is not defensible and calibration will challenge it.

## 3. Inputs

### 3.1 Self-review

Every rated employee writes one. Short by design — three prompts, no scoring:

1. What did you deliver this period, and what was the impact?
2. Where did you fall short of what you wanted, and what did you learn?
3. What do you want to be doing in twelve months, and what do you need?

Employees do **not** propose their own rating. That framing turns the self-review
into a negotiation and suppresses honest answers to question 2.

### 3.2 Peer feedback

Each employee is reviewed by **three to five peers**. The employee nominates up to
five names; the manager finalises the list and may add up to two reviewers from
partner teams. Additions are visible to the employee — there are no secret
reviewers. Feedback is shared back in **anonymised, aggregated** form, with
verbatim text used only where it cannot identify the author. Purely personal,
unspecific, or retaliatory feedback is discarded and reported to People Operations.

```yaml
peer_feedback:
  strengths: |
    What does this person do well that the team should keep relying on?
    Be specific. Name a project, a decision, a review.
  growth: |
    What is the single highest-value thing they could do differently
    next period? One item, not a list.
  # No rating field. Peers describe behaviour; managers rate.
```

### 3.3 Manager assessment

The manager writes the assessment and proposes the rating, drawing on the
self-review, peer feedback, delivery evidence, and their own observation. Every
rating needs at least two concrete examples; "strong quarter" is not evidence.

## 4. Calibration

Calibration is where ratings become comparable. It is mandatory: a rating that has
not been through calibration is a draft, not a rating.

### 4.1 How sessions run

- Held by function, chaired by the function head, with a People Operations
  facilitator. Managers present each report briefly: level, proposed rating, two
  supporting examples.
- Peers in the room challenge on **standard**, not personality. The question is
  always "would this be a 4 on my team?"
- The facilitator does not vote. Their job is to interrupt when discussion drifts
  to potential, likeability, tenure, visibility, or how much someone is paid.
- Ratings move up or down in the room, and the manager owns whatever comes out —
  "calibration made me do this" is not an acceptable thing to say.

### 4.2 Normalisation

Northwind does **not** operate a forced distribution. There is no quota and no
requirement to rate anyone a 1 or 2. There is a reference distribution used only
to spot outliers:

| Rating | Reference share |
|--------|-----------------|
| 5 | ~5% |
| 4 | ~20% |
| 3 | ~60% |
| 2 | ~10% |
| 1 | ~5% |

A team materially off this shape is asked to explain, not to change; a small,
strong team may legitimately land 40% at 4. A manager whose ratings are generous
or harsh in two consecutive cycles gets calibration coaching from People Operations.

### 4.3 Bias interrupts

Facilitators watch explicitly for **recency** (weighting the last six weeks over
the full period); **proximity** (rating in-office people above fully-remote peers,
checked against work location every cycle — see remote-work-policy.md); **leave
penalty** (rating down someone who took parental or extended sick leave); and
**level confusion** (rating an L3 against L5 expectations, or the reverse).

## 5. From Rating to Pay

Managers should understand the link but must not communicate a compensation number
before calibration closes.

- The merit multiplier is a function of **rating and compa-ratio**. Both inputs
  matter: a 4 at the top of band moves less than a 4 in the lower half.
- Bonus payout applies an individual factor derived from the spring rating.
- An employee on an active performance improvement plan is not eligible for a
  bonus payout or for promotion.
- Band minimum corrections are applied regardless of rating and are not funded
  from the merit pool.

All band, pool, and grid mechanics live in compensation-policy.md. Do not restate
them from memory in a review conversation; open the policy.

## 6. Delivering the Review

Live conversation, 1:1, direct manager — never in writing alone, never in a group
setting, never by proxy. The written review is shared **at or after** the
conversation, never before. In the spring cycle the pay outcome is delivered in
the same conversation, after the rating, not before. Book a follow-up 1:1 within a
week so the employee can react once they have read the document properly.
Escalations go to People Operations, and employees may attach a written response
to their own review, which is retained with it.

## 7. Performance Improvement Plans

A PIP is a **60-day** structured intervention, used where performance is at rating
1, or sustained at rating 2 with no improvement after documented feedback.

### 7.1 Before a PIP

A PIP is never the first step. There must first be documented verbal feedback on
the gap on at least two occasions, a written summary sent to the employee, and a
reasonable period — normally one cycle — to respond. People Operations must
approve every PIP before delivery; one opened without that review is invalid.

### 7.2 Structure

| Day | Milestone |
|-----|-----------|
| Day 0 | Delivery meeting: manager, employee, People Operations. Written plan handed over |
| Day 1–60 | Weekly 1:1 with written notes shared with the employee within 24 hours |
| Day 30 | Formal midpoint review: on track, off track, or at risk. Documented |
| Day 60 | Decision meeting |

The written plan must state, in specifics a third party could evaluate: the gap
with dated examples; the standard required, as observable outcomes rather than
attitudes; the support Northwind will provide; the measurement method and who
assesses it; and the consequence of not meeting the standard, stated plainly.

### 7.3 Outcomes

At day 60, exactly one of three things happens:

- **Successful completion.** The PIP closes in writing. The employee is
  immediately eligible again for promotion and bonus. There is no shadow record
  and no informal "still on watch" status.
- **Extension.** Once only, up to 30 additional days, where progress is real but
  incomplete. Requires People Operations approval.
- **Separation.** Managed by People Operations, not by the manager alone.

An employee may not be placed on a second PIP for the same gap within 12 months of
successfully completing one.

### 7.4 During a PIP

Not eligible for promotion or bonus payout. Still eligible for all leave,
benefits, and stipends — a PIP never restricts access to sick leave, parental
leave, or the employee assistance program. Equity continues to vest normally.

## 8. Documentation Requirements

| Artifact | Retained |
|----------|----------|
| Self-review | Yes |
| Manager written assessment | Yes |
| Final calibrated rating | Yes |
| Aggregated peer feedback | Yes |
| Employee's written response, if any | Yes |
| Raw attributed peer submissions | No — discarded after aggregation |
| Calibration discussion notes | No — outcomes only are recorded |

Retention periods for employee records follow the schedule maintained by Legal;
contact Rina Malhotra, Privacy Counsel, before deleting or exporting any of it,
including for a departing employee.

For managers: write feedback down when it happens, not in February — a private
running document per report is the highest-leverage habit at Northwind. Never
record performance concerns in a channel or ticket visible to the team, and never
store them outside approved systems. Hand notes to a successor via People Operations.
